Welcome. My name is **Andrea Vassallo**, and this is my Atlas.
## What is this Atlas
This is my digital garden: a set of linked notes where I write down what I learn and think about in the four areas of my work. Team leadership, e-commerce strategy, software architecture, and fullstack engineering.

It is the closest thing I have to a public record of how I think, what I care about, and where I am going.
## Who I am
I am an Engagement Manager at [**Nebulab**](https://www.nebulab.com "https://www.nebulab.com"), where I work with DTC and e-commerce brands on their technical and strategic problems.

I sit between the development team and the client. My job is to understand why we are building something before we build it, and to make sure every technical decision serves a business goal. Writing code is part of it. Most of the work is the conversation around it.

A bio can only say so much about how someone thinks. If you want the real picture, browse the [waypoints](/waypoints "/waypoints") and follow the links between them.
## Why an Atlas
Reading is not enough. I can go through hundreds of articles, books, and case studies, but if I never stop to organize what I read, most of it stays a vague feeling I cannot use.

This Atlas is how I pull those ideas out. Writing a note forces me to turn an intuition into something concrete, connected to other notes, and reusable later. It is how I move from "I think I understand this" to "I know I understand this".

Every waypoint has a maturity state. Some are seeds, which I call sketches. Others are frameworks I have worked on for a while. All of them link to each other, because the problems I work on are not isolated. E-commerce strategy shapes software architecture. Leadership decisions shape technical outcomes. Everything links.
